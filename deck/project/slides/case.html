Open on the distinction, because it is the first thing a CFO will test. The $147 to 172 million is the prize. It is what a one percent churn reduction on the subscriber base is worth in a year. I am not claiming this system delivers it. I am claiming it is the layer that lets the retention programme run on automated decisions you can audit, reproduce and defend in front of a regulator. The $74,000 is what this actually saves, in inference cost, by keeping two thirds of traffic on-prem. Against $17.6 billion of revenue that is a rounding error, and I would rather say so than let somebody find it. The cost argument that genuinely holds is optionality: when a provider raises prices or a cheaper model ships, it is a config line, not a release. On the four splits at the bottom. You asked for dollars by region and by plan type. Those splits are not published — reporting stopped after the private sale — so putting a number against them would be inventing it. What is solid is the user base and the migration journey itself, and that is what the POC is anchored to.

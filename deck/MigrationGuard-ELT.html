MigrationGuard · AI quality & governance control plane

The prize and the saving are two different numbers.
Only one of them belongs to this programme.

The prize

$147–172M

Annual revenue retained by a 1% reduction in churn — 82,000 households of 8.2M, at $1,800–2,100 ARPU.

This control plane does not reduce churn. It is what lets the retention programme run on automated decisions you can audit and defend.

The saving

$74K/yr

Inference cost avoided by serving 67% of 12.3M contacts on-prem, at realistic token volume.

The weakest of the three drivers against $17.6B revenue. The cost argument that holds is optionality, not price.

To production grade, with 2 engineers and 1 QA lead. This POC was built in 3 days on a two-core laptop.

What it buys

  • Personal data never leaves. It is a hard constraint evaluated before any cost or latency optimisation, and the gate fails closed — classifier down means cloud refused, not cloud unchecked.
  • Safety without an outage. Failing closed degrades to on-prem rather than returning an error.
  • One config line swaps a model. No release, no code change, and the compliance behaviour is unaffected by the swap.
  • Every decision is auditable: who answered, which zone, which guard fired, what it cost — refusals included.

What it still costs

  • Two controls overlapped and the cruder one silently vetoed the precise one — blocking 66% of customers who gave their name. Found by a load test, not by 133 unit tests.
  • The PII classifier is the ceiling at 2.9 requests a second, single process. Stateless, so it scales with replicas.
  • In-process tests never check the deployment. A stale gateway answered attacks for eight hours while every test passed.
  • An output-guard rejection has no retry path. The customer gets a 502. Known and unfixed.

Flat throughput against linearly rising latency is the signature of a serialised dependency. Presidio alone answers in 347ms — 2.9 req/s — and runs on every request. The ceiling belongs to the classifier, not the gateway, and it is stateless, so it scales with replicas.

The 12 that remain are each categorised and either fixed or consciously accepted with reasoning. Loosening the pattern that accounts for seven of them would flag an ordinary sentence, and this guard blocks — a false alarm is a real customer receiving an error.

Defect 1 · the deployment, not the code

A gateway left running from earlier in the day predated the guard wiring and answered “ignore all previous instructions” for eight hours, while every in-process test passed. Fixed by a preflight that asks the live service what it loaded and sends one real attack through it.

Defect 2 · the safety model blocked real customers

8 of 12 ordinary messages carrying a name or address got HTTP 400, all category S7 “Privacy”. The PII classifier already owned that decision. S7 made advisory: still audited, no longer blocking. Attacks caught: unchanged.

Load figures are from a Python harness, not k6 — k6 was not installed on this host. The same three scenarios ship as a k6 script with thresholds set from this baseline. Two CPU cores, no GPU: the shapes transfer, the absolute numbers do not.

Where this goes next

Eight weeks to production grade. Then the hybrid build this POC is deliberately not pretending to be.

Now · built, measured, runnable

  • Policy-driven routing with PII as a hard constraint, failing closed
  • Four models, per-model guardrails and fallback chains
  • 1,000-attack red team plus an AI-generated corpus, both re-runnable
  • Promotion gate on the PII detector, blocking two of three candidates
  • Three-scenario load test and a deployment preflight
  • 133 tests, 23 of 23 scenarios traced

8 weeks · 2 engineers + 1 QA lead

  • GPU inference nodes; classifier replicated to lift the 2.9 req/s ceiling
  • k6 in CI with thresholds from this baseline, including the zero-PII-to-cloud assertion
  • garak and PyRIT nightly, with this harness as the fast gate in front
  • Retry and canned-template fallback on an output-guard rejection
  • Kyverno admission: PII workloads cannot schedule outside the on-prem zone
  • Evaluation dataset rewritten by a second author

Then · the genuine hybrid

  • Managed Kubernetes for burst; on-prem holds steady state and all PII workloads
  • Workload identity and OIDC federation — no long-lived keys crossing the boundary
  • Private Link and mTLS through a service mesh
  • GitOps: one repository, two targets, the split declarative
  • OPA promoted to the policy engine; one model registry read from both sides
  • Per-segment cost attribution, and multi-region failover with stated RTO and RPO

Build versus buy

The gateway is commodity — LiteLLM, Portkey, Kong AI Gateway, Bedrock. I would buy the gateway and build the policy and evidence layer, because that is the part nobody sells and the part a regulator asks about.

What this is not

Not a replacement for your data science platform. This is the quality and governance control plane that sits in front of whatever models you already have, and it is model-agnostic by construction.

Not covered, and said plainly: no multi-turn attacks, the output path is lightly tested, garak and PyRIT were not run, and the evaluation dataset was written by the same person who wrote the guards.
